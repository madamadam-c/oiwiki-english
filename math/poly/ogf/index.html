    Ordinary Generating Functions

    The ordinary generating function (OGF) of a sequence 𝑎a is defined as a formal power series:

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛𝑎𝑛𝑥𝑛F(x)=∑nanxn

    𝑎a can be either a finite sequence or an infinite sequence. Common examples (assuming 𝑎a starts at 0):

    1. The ordinary generating function of sequence 𝑎 =⟨1,2,3⟩a=⟨1,2,3⟩ is 1 +2𝑥 +3𝑥21+2x+3x2.
    2. The ordinary generating function of sequence 𝑎 =⟨1,1,1,⋯⟩a=⟨1,1,1,⋯⟩ is ∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛∑n≥0xn.
    3. The generating function of sequence 𝑎 =⟨1,2,4,8,16,⋯⟩a=⟨1,2,4,8,16,⋯⟩ is ∑𝑛≥02𝑛𝑥𝑛∑n≥02nxn.
    4. The generating function of sequence 𝑎 =⟨1,3,5,7,9,⋯⟩a=⟨1,3,5,7,9,⋯⟩ is ∑𝑛≥0(2𝑛 +1)𝑥𝑛∑n≥0(2n+1)xn.

    In other words, if sequence 𝑎a has a general term formula, the coefficients of its ordinary generating function are that formula.

    Basic Operations

    Consider two sequences 𝑎,𝑏a,b with ordinary generating functions 𝐹(𝑥),𝐺(𝑥)F(x),G(x) respectively. Then:

    𝐹(𝑥)±𝐺(𝑥)=∑𝑛(𝑎𝑛±𝑏𝑛)𝑥𝑛F(x)±G(x)=∑n(an±bn)xn

    Therefore, 𝐹(𝑥) ±𝐺(𝑥)F(x)±G(x) is the ordinary generating function of sequence ⟨𝑎𝑛 ±𝑏𝑛⟩⟨an±bn⟩.

    Consider multiplication, which is convolution:

    𝐹(𝑥)𝐺(𝑥)=∑𝑛𝑥𝑛𝑛∑𝑖=0𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑛−𝑖F(x)G(x)=∑nxn∑i=0naibn−i

    Therefore, 𝐹(𝑥)𝐺(𝑥)F(x)G(x) is the ordinary generating function of sequence ⟨∑𝑛𝑖=0𝑎𝑖𝑏𝑛−𝑖⟩⟨∑i=0naibn−i⟩.

    Closed Form

    In the process of using generating functions, we will not always use the form of formal power series; we will transform to closed form when appropriate to simplify.

    For example, for the ordinary generating function 𝐹(𝑥) =∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛F(x)=∑n≥0xn of ⟨1,1,1,⋯⟩⟨1,1,1,⋯⟩, we can find:

    𝐹(𝑥)𝑥+1=𝐹(𝑥)F(x)x+1=F(x)

    Then solving this equation:

    𝐹(𝑥)=11−𝑥F(x)=11−x

    This is the closed form of ∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛∑n≥0xn.

    Consider the generating function 𝐹(𝑥) =∑𝑛≥0𝑝𝑛𝑥𝑛F(x)=∑n≥0pnxn of the geometric progression ⟨1,𝑝,𝑝2,𝑝3,𝑝4,⋯⟩⟨1,p,p2,p3,p4,⋯⟩:

    𝐹(𝑥)𝑝𝑥+1=𝐹(𝑥)𝐹(𝑥)=11−𝑝𝑥F(x)px+1=F(x)F(x)=11−px

    The closed form of a geometric progression and its expansion form are commonly used transformation techniques.

    Small Exercises

    Find the ordinary generating functions (both in formal power series form and closed form) of the following sequences. The difficulty is progressive.

    1. 𝑎 =⟨0,1,1,1,1,⋯⟩a=⟨0,1,1,1,1,⋯⟩.
    2. 𝑎 =⟨1,0,1,0,1,⋯⟩a=⟨1,0,1,0,1,⋯⟩.
    3. 𝑎 =⟨1,2,3,4,⋯⟩a=⟨1,2,3,4,⋯⟩.
    4. 𝑎𝑛 =(𝑚𝑛)an=(mn) (𝑚m is a constant, 𝑛 ≥0n≥0).
    5. 𝑎𝑛 =(𝑚+𝑛𝑛)an=(m+nn) (𝑚m is a constant, 𝑛 ≥0n≥0).
    Answers

    First:

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛≥1𝑥𝑛=𝑥1−𝑥F(x)=∑n≥1xn=x1−x

    Second:

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛≥0𝑥2𝑛=∑𝑛≥0(𝑥2)𝑛=11−𝑥2F(x)=∑n≥0x2n=∑n≥0(x2)n=11−x2

    Third (via differentiation):

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛≥0(𝑛+1)𝑥𝑛=∑𝑛≥1𝑛𝑥𝑛−1=∑𝑛≥0(𝑥𝑛)′=(11−𝑥)′=1(1−𝑥)2F(x)=∑n≥0(n+1)xn=∑n≥1nxn−1=∑n≥0(xn)′=(11−x)′=1(1−x)2

    Fourth (binomial theorem):

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛≥0(𝑚𝑛)𝑥𝑛=(1+𝑥)𝑚F(x)=∑n≥0(mn)xn=(1+x)m

    Fifth:

    𝐹(𝑥)=∑𝑛≥0(𝑚+𝑛𝑛)𝑥𝑛=1(1−𝑥)𝑚+1F(x)=∑n≥0(m+nn)xn=1(1−x)m+1

    Can be proved by induction.

    First, when 𝑚 =0m=0, we have 𝐹(𝑥) =11−𝑥F(x)=11−x.

    And when 𝑚 >0m>0:

    1(1−𝑥)𝑚+1=1(1−𝑥)𝑚11−𝑥=(∑𝑛≥0(𝑚+𝑛−1𝑛)𝑥𝑛)(∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛)=∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛𝑛∑𝑖=0(𝑚+𝑖−1𝑖)=∑𝑛≥0(𝑚+𝑛𝑛)𝑥𝑛1(1−x)m+1=1(1−x)m11−x=(∑n≥0(m+n−1n)xn)(∑n≥0xn)=∑n≥0xn∑i=0n(m+i−1i)=∑n≥0(m+nn)xn

    Generating Function of Fibonacci Sequence

    Now we derive the generating function of the Fibonacci sequence.

    The Fibonacci sequence is defined as 𝑎0 =0,𝑎1 =1,𝑎𝑛 =𝑎𝑛−1 +𝑎𝑛−2 (𝑛 >1)a0=0,a1=1,an=an−1+an−2(n>1). Let its ordinary generating function be 𝐹(𝑥)F(x). According to its recurrence relation, we can similarly list an equation for 𝐹(𝑥)F(x):

    𝐹(𝑥)=𝑥𝐹(𝑥)+𝑥2𝐹(𝑥)−𝑎0𝑥+𝑎1𝑥+𝑎0F(x)=xF(x)+x2F(x)−a0x+a1x+a0

    Solving:

    𝐹(𝑥)=𝑥1−𝑥−𝑥2F(x)=x1−x−x2

    The next question is, how to find its expanded form?

    Expansion Method 1

    Consider 𝑥 +𝑥2x+x2 as a whole:

    𝐹(𝑥)=𝑥1−(𝑥+𝑥2)=𝑥∞∑𝑘=0(𝑥+𝑥2)𝑘=𝑥∞∑𝑘=0𝑘∑𝑖=0(𝑘𝑖)𝑥𝑘−𝑖(𝑥2)𝑖=∞∑𝑘=0𝑘∑𝑖=0(𝑘𝑖)𝑥𝑘+𝑖+1=∞∑𝑛=1⌊(𝑛−1)/2⌋∑𝑖=0(𝑛−𝑖−1𝑖)𝑥𝑛.F(x)=x1−(x+x2)=x∑k=0∞(x+x2)k=x∑k=0∞∑i=0k(ki)xk−i(x2)i=∑k=0∞∑i=0k(ki)xk+i+1=∑n=1∞∑i=0⌊(n−1)/2⌋(n−i−1i)xn.

    In the last step, let 𝑛 =𝑘 +𝑖 +1n=k+i+1 and change the order of summation. From this, we can get the general term formula:

    𝑎𝑛=⌊(𝑛−1)/2⌋∑𝑖=0(𝑛−𝑖−1𝑖).an=∑i=0⌊(n−1)/2⌋(n−i−1i).

    This is not the form we know involving the golden ratio.

    Expansion Method 2

    Consider solving an equation with undetermined coefficients:

    𝐴1−𝑎𝑥+𝐵1−𝑏𝑥=𝑥1−𝑥−𝑥2A1−ax+B1−bx=x1−x−x2

    After combining denominators:

    𝐴−𝐴𝑏𝑥+𝐵−𝑎𝐵𝑥(1−𝑎𝑥)(1−𝑏𝑥)=𝑥1−𝑥−𝑥2A−Abx+B−aBx(1−ax)(1−bx)=x1−x−x2

    Equating coefficients of corresponding terms:

    ⎧{ { {⎨{ { {⎩𝐴+𝐵=0−𝐴𝑏−𝑎𝐵=1𝑎+𝑏=1𝑎𝑏=−1{A+B=0−Ab−aB=1a+b=1ab=−1

    Solving:

    ⎧{ { { {⎨{ { { {⎩𝐴=1√5𝐵=−1√5𝑎=1+√52𝑏=1−√52{A=15B=−15a=1+52b=1−52

    Then, based on the expansion form of geometric progressions, we can get the general term formula of the Fibonacci sequence:

    𝑥1−𝑥−𝑥2=∑𝑛≥0𝑥𝑛1√5((1+√52)𝑛−(1−√52)𝑛)x1−x−x2=∑n≥0xn15((1+52)n−(1−52)n)

    This is also called another closed form of the Fibonacci sequence (𝑥1−𝑥−𝑥2x1−x−x2 is a closed form).

    For any polynomials 𝑃(𝑥),𝑄(𝑥)P(x),Q(x), the expanded form of the generating function 𝑃(𝑥)𝑄(𝑥)P(x)Q(x) can be found using the above method. In practical application, we often first find the roots of 𝑄(𝑥)Q(x), express the denominator as ∏(1 −𝑝𝑖𝑥)𝑑𝑖∏(1−pix)di, and then find the numerator.

    When there are repeated roots after factoring the denominator, we need one more partial fraction for each repeated root. Consider finding the general term formula for coefficients of the generating function

    𝐺(𝑥)=1(1−𝑥)(1−2𝑥)2G(x)=1(1−x)(1−2x)2

    We have:

    𝐺(𝑥)=𝑐01−𝑥+𝑐11−2𝑥+𝑐2(1−2𝑥)2G(x)=c01−x+c11−2x+c2(1−2x)2

    Solving:
