    Congruence Equations

    Definition

    Congruence Equation

    For a positive integer 𝑚m and a univariate polynomial with integer coefficients 𝑓(𝑥) =∑𝑛𝑖=0𝑎𝑖𝑥𝑖f(x)=∑i=0naixi, where the unknown 𝑥 ∈𝐙𝑚x∈Zm, an equation of the form

    𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑚)(1)(1)f(x)≡0(modm)

    is called a univariate congruence equation (Congruence Equation) in modulus 𝑚m for the unknown 𝑥x.

    If 𝑎𝑛 ≢0(mod𝑚)an≢0(modm), then the above equation is called an 𝑛n-degree congruence equation.

    Congruence equation systems can be defined similarly.

    For content about linear congruence equations and systems, please refer to Linear Congruence Equation and Chinese Remainder Theorem.

    This article first studies the solvability and solution set structure of congruence equations, then briefly introduces methods for solving higher-degree congruence equations.

    As can be known from the Chinese Remainder Theorem, solving congruence equations with composite modulus 𝑚m can be reduced to solving prime power modulus cases. Therefore, the following only introduces theories related to prime power modulus congruence equations and prime modulus congruence equations.

    Prime Power Modulus Congruence Equations

    Assume here that the modulus 𝑚 =𝑝𝑒 (𝑝 ∈𝐏, 𝑒 ∈𝐙>1)m=pe (p∈P, e∈Z>1).

    Note that if 𝑥0x0 is a solution of the equation

    𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x)≡0(modpe)

    then 𝑥0x0 is also a solution of the equation

    𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒−1).f(x)≡0(modpe−1).

    This inspires us to try to construct solutions for higher modulus powers from solutions for lower modulus powers. We have the following theorem:

    Theorem 1 (Hensel's Lemma)

    For a prime 𝑝p and integer 𝑒 >1e>1, let 𝑓(𝑥) =∑𝑛𝑖=0𝑎𝑖𝑥𝑖 (𝑝𝑒 ∤𝑎𝑛)f(x)=∑i=0naixi (pe∤an) be a polynomial with integer coefficients, and let 𝑓′(𝑥) =∑𝑛𝑖=1𝑖𝑎𝑖𝑥𝑖−1f′(x)=∑i=1niaixi−1 be its derivative. Let 𝑥0x0 be a solution of the equation

    𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒−1)(2)(2)f(x)≡0(modpe−1)

    Then:

    1. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≢0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≢0(modp), then there exists an integer 𝑡t such that

      𝑥=𝑥0+𝑝𝑒−1𝑡(3)(3)x=x0+pe−1t

      is a solution of the equation

      𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)(4)(4)f(x)≡0(modpe)

    2. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≡0(modp) and 𝑓(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0)≡0(modpe), then for 𝑡 =0,1,…,𝑝 −1t=0,1,…,p−1, the 𝑥x determined by equation (3)(3) are all solutions of equation (4)(4).

    3. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≡0(modp) and 𝑓(𝑥0) ≢0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0)≢0(modpe), then no solution of equation (4)(4) can be constructed from equation (3)(3).
    Proof

    We assume equation (3)(3) is a solution of equation (4)(4), i.e.,

    𝑓(𝑥0+𝑝𝑒−1𝑡)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0+pe−1t)≡0(modpe)

    After rearrangement, we get

    𝑓(𝑥0)+𝑝𝑒−1𝑡𝑓′(𝑥0)≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0)+pe−1tf′(x0)≡0(modpe)

    Thus

    𝑡𝑓′(𝑥0)≡−𝑓(𝑥0)𝑝𝑒−1(mod𝑝)(5)(5)tf′(x0)≡−f(x0)pe−1(modp)
    1. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≢0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≢0(modp), then equation (5)(5) in 𝑡t has a unique solution 𝑡0t0, and substituting into equation (3)(3) verifies that it is a solution of equation (4)(4).
    2. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≡0(modp) and 𝑓(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0)≡0(modpe), then any 𝑡t satisfies equation (5)(5), and substituting into equation (3)(3) verifies that they are all solutions of equation (4)(4).
    3. If 𝑓′(𝑥0) ≡0(mod𝑝)f′(x0)≡0(modp) and 𝑓(𝑥0) ≢0(mod𝑝𝑒)f(x0)≢0(modpe), then equation (5)(5) has no solution, so no solution of equation (4)(4) can be constructed from equation (3)(3).

    We then have the following corollary:

    Corollary 1

    For the 𝑝p, 𝑒e, 𝑓(𝑥)f(x), 𝑥0x0 of Theorem 1:

    1. If 𝑠s is a solution of equation 𝑓(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)f(x)≡0(modp) and 𝑓′(𝑠) ≢0(mod𝑝)f′(s)≢0(modp), then there exists 𝑥𝑠 ∈𝐙𝑝𝑒xs∈Zpe, 𝑥𝑠 ≡𝑠(mod𝑝)xs≡s(modp) such that 𝑥𝑠xs is a solution of equation (4)(4).
    2. If equation 𝑓(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)f(x)≡0(modp) and equation 𝑓′(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)f′(x)≡0(modp) have no common solutions, then equation (4)(4) and equation 𝑓(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)f(x)≡0(modp) have the same number of solutions.

    Thus we can reduce prime power modulus congruence equations to prime modulus congruence equations.

    Prime Modulus Congruence Equations

    Let 𝑝 ∈𝐏p∈P here, and 𝑓(𝑥) =∑𝑛𝑖=0𝑎𝑖𝑥𝑖f(x)=∑i=0naixi be a polynomial with integer coefficients, where 𝑝 ∤𝑎𝑛p∤an, 𝑥 ∈𝐙𝑝x∈Zp.

    Theorem 2

    If equation

    𝑓(𝑥)≡0(mod𝑝)(6)(6)f(x)≡0(modp)

    has 𝑘k distinct solutions 𝑥1,𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑘 (𝑘 ≤𝑛)x1,x2,…,xk (k≤n), then

    𝑓(𝑥)≡𝑔(𝑥)𝑘∏𝑖=1(𝑥−𝑥𝑖)(mod𝑝),f(x)≡g(x)∏i=1k(x−xi)(modp),

    where deg⁡𝑔 =𝑛 −𝑘deg⁡g=n−k and [𝑥𝑛−𝑘]𝑔(𝑥) =𝑎𝑛[xn−k]g(x)=an.

    Proof

    Apply mathematical induction on 𝑘k.

    • When 𝑘 =1k=1, by polynomial division with remainder, we have 𝑓(𝑥) =(𝑥 −𝑥1)𝑔(𝑥) +𝑟f(x)=(x−x1)g(x)+r, where 𝑟 ∈𝐙r∈Z.

      From 𝑓(𝑥1) ≡0(mod𝑝)f(x1)≡0(modp), we know 𝑟 ≡0(mod𝑝)r≡0(modp), thus 𝑓(𝑥) ≡(𝑥 −𝑥1)𝑔(𝑥)(mod𝑝)f(x)≡(x−x1)g(x)(modp).

    • Assume the proposition holds for 𝑘 −1k−1 (𝑘 >1k>1). Now let 𝑓(𝑥)f(x) have 𝑘k distinct solutions 𝑥1,𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑘x1,x2,…,xk, then 𝑓(𝑥) ≡(𝑥 −𝑥1)ℎ(𝑥)(mod𝑝)f(x)≡(x−x1)h(x)(modp). Furthermore, for all 𝑖 =2,3,…,𝑘i=2,3,…,k, we have

      0≡𝑓(𝑥𝑖)≡(𝑥𝑖−𝑥1)ℎ(𝑥𝑖)(mod𝑝)0≡f(xi)≡(xi−x1)h(xi)(modp)

      Thus ℎ(𝑥)h(x) has 𝑘 −1k−1 distinct solutions 𝑥2,𝑥3,…,𝑥𝑘x2,x3,…,xk, and by the induction hypothesis,

      ℎ(𝑥)≡𝑔(𝑥)𝑘∏𝑖=2(𝑥−𝑥𝑖)(mod𝑝)h(x)≡g(x)∏i=2k(x−xi)(modp)

      where deg⁡𝑔 =𝑛 −𝑘deg⁡g=n−k and [𝑥𝑛−𝑘]𝑔(𝑥) =𝑎𝑛[xn−k]g(x)=an.

      Therefore, the proposition is proved.

    Corollary 2

    For prime 𝑝p,

    • (∀𝑥 ∈𝐙),  𝑥𝑝−1 −1 ≡∏𝑝−1𝑖=1(𝑥 −𝑖)(mod𝑝)(∀x∈Z),  xp−1−1≡∏i=1p−1(x−i)(modp).
    • (Wilson's Theorem) (𝑝 −1)! ≡ −1(mod𝑝)(p−1)!≡−1(modp).

    Theorem 3 (Lagrange's Theorem)

    Equation (6)(6) has at most 𝑛n distinct solutions.

    Proof

    Assume 𝑓(𝑥)f(x) has 𝑛 +1n+1 distinct solutions 𝑥1,𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑛+1x1,x2,…,xn+1, then by Theorem 2, for 𝑥1,𝑥2,…,𝑥𝑛x1,x2,…,xn we have

    𝑓(𝑥)≡𝑎𝑛𝑛∏𝑖=1(𝑥−𝑥𝑖)(mod𝑝)f(x)≡an∏i=1n(x−xi)(modp)

    Let 𝑥 =𝑥𝑛+1x=xn+1, then

    0≡𝑓(𝑥𝑛+1)≡𝑎𝑛𝑛∏𝑖=1(𝑥𝑛+1−𝑥𝑖)(mod𝑝)0≡f(xn+1)≡an∏i=1n(xn+1−xi)(modp)

    But the right side is clearly not a multiple of 𝑝p, so the assumption leads to a contradiction.

    Corollary 3

    If the congruence equation ∑𝑛𝑖=0𝑏𝑖𝑥𝑖 ≡0(mod𝑝)∑i=0nbixi≡0(modp) has more than 𝑛n solutions, then

    (∀𝑖=0,1,…,𝑛),  𝑝∣𝑏𝑖.(∀i=0,1,…,n),  p∣bi.

    Theorem 4

    If the number of solutions of equation (6)(6) is not 𝑝p, then there exists a polynomial 𝑟(𝑥)r(x) with integer coefficients satisfying deg⁡𝑟 <𝑝deg⁡r<p such that the solution sets of 𝑓(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)f(x)≡0(modp) and 𝑟(𝑥) ≡0(mod𝑝)r(x)≡0(modp) are identical.

    Proof

    Assume 𝑛 ≥𝑝n≥p, perform polynomial division on 𝑓(𝑥)f(x)
