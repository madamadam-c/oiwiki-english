    Numerical Characteristics of Random Variables

    This article introduces the expectation, variance, and other numerical characteristics of random variables.

    Expectation

    Definition

    Discrete Random Variable

    Let 𝑋X be a discrete random variable with probability distribution 𝑝𝑖 =𝑃{𝑋 =𝑥𝑖}pi=P{X=xi}. If the sum

    ∑𝑥𝑖𝑝𝑖∑xipi

    converges absolutely, its value is called the expectation of 𝑋X, denoted 𝐸𝑋EX.

    Continuous Random Variable

    Let 𝑋X be a continuous random variable with density function 𝑓(𝑥)f(x). If the integral

    ∫ℝ𝑥𝑓(𝑥)d𝑥∫Rxf(x)dx

    converges absolutely, its value is called the expectation of 𝑋X, denoted 𝐸𝑋EX.

    Unified Definition

    Let 𝑋X be a random variable with distribution function 𝐹(𝑥)F(x). If the Stieltjes integral

    ∫ℝ𝑥d𝐹(𝑥)∫RxdF(x)

    converges absolutely, its value is called the expectation of 𝑋X, denoted 𝐸𝑋EX.

    Example of Non-existent Expectation

    Consider a discrete random variable 𝑋X with the following distribution

    𝑃{𝑋=(−1)𝑘2𝑘𝑘}=12𝑘,𝑘=1,2,⋯P{X=(−1)k2kk}=12k,k=1,2,⋯

    Although the sum ∑𝑥𝑖𝑝𝑖∑xipi converges to −ln⁡2−ln⁡2, since it does not converge absolutely, the expectation of 𝑋X does not exist.

    Consider a continuous random variable 𝑌Y with the following density function

    𝑓(𝑦)=1𝜋⋅11+𝑦2,𝑦∈(−∞,+∞)f(y)=1π⋅11+y2,y∈(−∞,+∞)

    It is easy to verify that the expectation of 𝑌Y also does not exist.

    Properties of Expectation

    Linearity

    If random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y have existing expectations, then

    • For any real numbers 𝑎,𝑏a,b, 𝐸(𝑎𝑋 +𝑏) =𝑎 ⋅𝐸𝑋 +𝑏E(aX+b)=a⋅EX+b.
    • 𝐸(𝑋 +𝑌) =𝐸𝑋 +𝐸𝑌E(X+Y)=EX+EY.

    Expectation of Product of Random Variables

    If random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y have existing expectations and 𝑋,𝑌X,Y are independent, then

    𝐸(𝑋𝑌)=𝐸𝑋⋅𝐸𝑌E(XY)=EX⋅EY

    Note: The independence in the above property is not a necessary condition.

    Counterexample

    Consider random variables 𝑋X and 𝑌Y, where 𝑋X follows a uniform distribution on [ −1,1][−1,1], and 𝑌 =𝑋2Y=X2.

    Converting Expectation and Probability

    For a random event 𝐴A, consider its indicator function 𝐼𝐴IA:

    𝐼𝐴(𝜔)={1,𝜔∈𝐴0,𝜔∉𝐴IA(ω)={1,ω∈A0,ω∉A

    According to the definition, its expectation is 𝐸𝐼𝐴 =𝑃(𝐴)EIA=P(A). This conversion is very common in practical applications.

    Suppose for a sequence {𝑎𝑖}{ai} of length 𝑛n, 𝑎𝑘ak takes value 𝑘k with probability 𝑝𝑘pk, and takes value 00 with probability 1 −𝑝𝑘1−pk. Consider how to find the expectation of 𝑆 =∑𝑛𝑖=1𝑎𝑖S=∑i=1nai.

    If we use the definition directly, we need to find the probability of 𝑆S at each possible value; this calculation process is quite tedious, so we will not elaborate.

    On the other hand, let 𝐼𝑘Ik be the indicator function of the random event 𝑎𝑘 =𝑘ak=k. Then

    𝑆=𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑘⋅𝐼𝑘S=∑k=1nk⋅Ik

    𝐸𝑆=𝐸(𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑘⋅𝐼𝑘)=𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑘⋅𝐸[𝐼𝑘]=𝑛∑𝑘=1𝑘⋅𝑝𝑘ES=E(∑k=1nk⋅Ik)=∑k=1nk⋅E[Ik]=∑k=1nk⋅pk

    Conditional Distribution and Conditional Expectation

    We have previously studied conditional probability; similarly, we can introduce the concept of conditional expectation.

    Definition

    For two random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y, the probability distribution (density function) of 𝑋X given 𝑌 =𝑦Y=y is called the conditional probability distribution (conditional probability density), denoted respectively as

    𝑃(𝑋=𝑥𝑖|𝑌=𝑦)𝑓𝑋|𝑌(𝑥|𝑦)P(X=xi|Y=y)fX|Y(x|y)

    Under this condition, the expectation of 𝑋X is called the conditional expectation, denoted 𝐸[𝑋|𝑌 =𝑦]E[X|Y=y].

    Properties of Conditional Expectation

    Many properties of conditional expectation can be derived from conditional probability, so they will not be elaborated here.

    It is worth mentioning that 𝐸[𝑋|𝑌]E[X|Y] is generally a function of the random variable 𝑌Y, and this function is usually not linear. But in fact,

    𝐸[𝐸[𝑋|𝑌]]=𝐸𝑋E[E[X|Y]]=EX

    This is called the law of total expectation.

    Application

    HDU 5984 Pocky

    There is a Pocky stick of length 𝐿L. Each time it is randomly broken into two pieces. If the length of the right piece is not greater than 𝑑d, stop; otherwise, repeat the above process on the right piece. Find the expected number of repetitions.

    Solution

    Let 𝑓(𝑥)f(x) denote the expected number of repetitions for a stick of length 𝑥x. The case 𝑥 ≤𝑑x≤d is trivial.

    When 𝑥 >𝑑x>d, suppose the break point is at a distance 𝑘k from the right end. Then 𝑘 ∼𝑈[0,𝑥]k∼U[0,x]. The expected number of repetitions is

    𝑔(𝑘)={1,𝑘≤𝑑1+𝑓(𝑘),𝑘>𝑑g(k)={1,k≤d1+f(k),k>d

    By the law of total expectation,

    𝑓(𝑥)=𝐸𝑔(𝑘)=1+1𝑥⋅∫𝑥𝑑𝑓(𝑡)d𝑡f(x)=Eg(k)=1+1x⋅∫dxf(t)dt

    Solving this integral equation and substituting the initial condition gives

    𝑓(𝑥)=1+ln⁡𝑥𝑑f(x)=1+ln⁡xd

    Variance

    Definition

    Let the expectation 𝐸𝑋EX of random variable 𝑋X exist, and let the expectation

    𝐸(𝑋−𝐸𝑋)2E(X−EX)2

    also exist. Then the value of the above expression is called the variance of random variable 𝑋X, denoted 𝐷𝑋DX or 𝑉𝑎𝑟(𝑥)Var(x). The arithmetic square root of variance is called the standard deviation, denoted 𝜎(𝑋) =√𝐷𝑋σ(X)=DX.

    Properties of Variance

    If the variance of random variable 𝑋X exists, then

    • For any constants 𝑎,𝑏a,b, 𝐷(𝑎𝑋 +𝑏) =𝑎2 ⋅𝐷𝑋D(aX+b)=a2⋅DX
    • 𝐷𝑋 =𝐸(𝑋2) −(𝐸𝑋)2DX=E(X2)−(EX)2

    Covariance and Correlation Coefficient

    In general, the equation 𝐷(𝑋 +𝑌) =𝐷𝑋 +𝐷𝑌D(X+Y)=DX+DY does not hold. We naturally ask two questions:

    • What exactly is the difference between 𝐷(𝑋 +𝑌)D(X+Y) and 𝐷𝑋 +𝐷𝑌DX+DY?
    • Under what circumstances are 𝐷(𝑋 +𝑌)D(X+Y) and 𝐷𝑋 +𝐷𝑌DX+DY equal?

    For the first question, we introduce covariance as the answer.

    Definition of Covariance

    For random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y, the quantity

    𝐸((𝑋−𝐸𝑋)(𝑌−𝐸𝑌))E((X−EX)(Y−EY))

    is called the covariance of 𝑋X and 𝑌Y, denoted Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌)Cov⁡(X,Y).

    Properties of Covariance

    For random variables 𝑋,𝑌,𝑍X,Y,Z:

    • Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌) =Cov⁡(𝑌,𝑋)Cov⁡(X,Y)=Cov⁡(Y,X)
    • For any constants 𝑎,𝑏a,b, Cov⁡(𝑎𝑋 +𝑏𝑌,𝑍) =𝑎 ⋅Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑍) +𝑏 ⋅Cov⁡(𝑌,𝑍)Cov⁡(aX+bY,Z)=a⋅Cov⁡(X,Z)+b⋅Cov⁡(Y,Z)

    Additionally, covariance and variance have the following relationship:

    • 𝐷𝑋 =Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑋)DX=Cov⁡(X,X)
    • 𝐷(𝑋 +𝑌) =𝐷𝑋 +2Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌) +𝐷𝑌D(X+Y)=DX+2Cov⁡(X,Y)+DY
    About Covariance

    You may notice that the properties of covariance are highly consistent in form with the operational properties of vector inner products.

    From the perspective of functional analysis, for a given probability space, all random variables on it form a linear space, and covariance is an inner product on this space. Standard deviation is the norm induced by this inner product.

    For the second question raised earlier, it is easy to see that 𝐷(𝑋 +𝑌) =𝐷𝑋 +𝐷𝑌D(X+Y)=DX+DY if and only if Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌) =0Cov⁡(X,Y)=0. An intuitive sufficient condition is that 𝑋X and 𝑌Y are independent, because in this case,

    Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌)=𝐸((𝑋−𝐸𝑋)(𝑌−𝐸𝑌))=𝐸(𝑋−𝐸𝑋)𝐸(𝑌−𝐸𝑌)=0Cov⁡(X,Y)=E((X−EX)(Y−EY))=E(X−EX)E(Y−EY)=0

    But this condition is not sufficient. To describe the relationship between random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y satisfying Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌) =0Cov⁡(X,Y)=0, we introduce the correlation coefficient.

    Correlation Coefficient

    For random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y, the quantity

    Cov⁡(𝑋,𝑌)𝜎(𝑋)𝜎(𝑌)Cov⁡(X,Y)σ(X)σ(Y)

    is called the Pearson correlation coefficient of 𝑋X and 𝑌Y, denoted 𝜌𝑋,𝑌ρX,Y.

    The Pearson correlation coefficient describes the strength of linear association between two random variables. The larger |𝜌𝑋,𝑌||ρX,Y|, the stronger the linear association between 𝑋X and 𝑌Y. It can be easily proven that |𝜌𝑋,𝑌| ≤1|ρX,Y|≤1, and |𝜌𝑋,𝑌| =1|ρX,Y|=1 can only occur in the following two situations:

    • When there exists a real number 𝑎a and a positive real number 𝑏b such that 𝑃(𝑋 =𝑎 +𝑏𝑌) =1P(X=a+bY)=1, we have 𝜌𝑋,𝑌 =1ρX,Y=1;
    • When there exists a real number 𝑎a and a negative real number 𝑏b such that 𝑃(𝑋 =𝑎 +𝑏𝑌) =1P(X=a+bY)=1, we have 𝜌𝑋,𝑌 = −1ρX,Y=−1.

    When 𝜌𝑋,𝑌 =0ρX,Y=0, we say random variables 𝑋X and 𝑌Y are uncorrelated, meaning there is no linear relationship between 𝑋X and 𝑌Y.

    Uncorrelated vs. Independent

    Two random variables being uncorrelated only indicates that there is no linear association between them, not that there are no other forms of relationship.

    Therefore, for two random variables 𝑋,𝑌X,Y, being uncorrelated is a necessary but not sufficient condition for them to be independent.
