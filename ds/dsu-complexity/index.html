    DSU Complexity

    This section is reproduced and modified from Time Complexity - A Brief Discussion of Potential Analysis, with permission from the original author.

    Definitions

    Ackermann Function

    First, define 𝛼(𝑛)α(n). To give this definition, first define 𝐴𝑘(𝑗)Ak(j).

    Define 𝐴𝑘(𝑗)Ak(j) as:

    𝐴𝑘(𝑗)={𝑗+1𝑘=0𝐴(𝑗+1)𝑘−1(𝑗)𝑘≥1Ak(j)={j+1k=0Ak−1(j+1)(j)k≥1

    This is the Ackermann function.

    Here, 𝑓𝑖(𝑥)fi(x) means applying 𝑓f to 𝑥x consecutively 𝑖i times, i.e. 𝑓0(𝑥) =𝑥f0(x)=x and 𝑓𝑖(𝑥) =𝑓(𝑓𝑖−1(𝑥))fi(x)=f(fi−1(x)).

    Then define 𝛼(𝑛)α(n) as the minimum integer value such that 𝐴𝛼(𝑛)(1) ≥𝑛Aα(n)(1)≥n. Note that we previously described it as 𝐴𝛼(𝑛)(𝛼(𝑛)) ≥𝑛Aα(n)(α(n))≥n; in any case, their growth rates are both very slow, and the value does not exceed 4.

    Basic Definitions

    Every node has a rank. Here, rank is not the number of nodes, but depth. A node's initial rank is 0. During union, if two nodes have different ranks, merge the node with smaller rank into the node with larger rank, and do not update the rank of the larger node. Otherwise, merge one node into the other arbitrarily and increase the root's rank by 1. The root node's rank gives the height of the tree. Let the rank of x be 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥)rnk(x), and similarly let the parent of x be 𝑓𝑎(𝑥)fa(x). We always have 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) +1 ≤𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑓𝑎(𝑥))rnk(x)+1≤rnk(fa(x)).

    To define the potential function, first define an auxiliary function 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)level(x), where 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥) =max(𝑘 :𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑓𝑎(𝑥)) ≥𝐴𝑘(𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥)))level(x)=max(k:rnk(fa(x))≥Ak(rnk(x))). When 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) ≥1rnk(x)≥1, define another auxiliary function 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥) =max(𝑖 :𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑓𝑎(𝑥)) ≥𝐴𝑖𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)(𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥))iter(x)=max(i:rnk(fa(x))≥Alevel(x)i(rnk(x)). These functions are defined for 𝑥x satisfying 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) >0rnk(x)>0 and where 𝑥x is not the root of a tree.

    The definitions above may be dizzying. Rephrased: for a pair of nodes 𝑥x and 𝑓𝑎(𝑥)fa(x), if 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) >0rnk(x)>0, we can always find a pair 𝑖,𝑘i,k such that 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑓𝑎(𝑥)) ≥𝐴𝑖𝑘(𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥))rnk(fa(x))≥Aki(rnk(x)). Then 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥) =max(𝑘)level(x)=max(k), and under this premise, 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥) =max(𝑖)iter(x)=max(i). 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙level describes the maximum iteration level of 𝐴A, while 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟iter describes the maximum number of iterations at that maximum level.

    For these two functions, 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)level(x) always increases or stays unchanged as operations proceed; if 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)level(x) does not increase, then 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥)iter(x) also only increases or stays unchanged. They always satisfy the following two inequalities:

    0≤𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)<𝛼(𝑛)0≤level(x)<α(n)1≤𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥)≤𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥)1≤iter(x)≤rnk(x)

    These are easy to prove by considering the definitions of 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)level(x), 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥)iter(x), and 𝐴𝑗𝑘Akj; we leave them to the reader to become familiar with the definitions.

    Define the potential function Φ(𝑆) =∑𝑥∈𝑆Φ(𝑥)Φ(S)=∑x∈SΦ(x), where 𝑆S denotes the whole DSU, and 𝑥x is a node in the DSU. Define Φ(𝑥)Φ(x) as:

    Φ(𝑥)={𝛼(𝑛)×rnk(𝑥)rnk(𝑥)=0 或 𝑥 为某棵树的根节点(𝛼(𝑛)−level(𝑥))×rnk(𝑥)−𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥)otherwiseΦ(x)={α(n)×rnk(x)rnk(x)=0 或 x 为某棵树的根节点(α(n)−level(x))×rnk(x)−iter(x)otherwise

    Then we prove the amortized time complexity Θ(𝛼(𝑛))Θ(α(n)) by analyzing the potential changes caused by operations. Note that the 𝑢𝑛𝑖𝑜𝑛(𝑥,𝑦)union(x,y) operation discussed here guarantees that both 𝑥x and 𝑦y are roots of some trees, so no additional 𝑓𝑖𝑛𝑑(𝑥)find(x) or 𝑓𝑖𝑛𝑑(𝑦)find(y) is needed.

    We can see that the potential is always nonnegative. Also, at the beginning, the DSU's potential is 00.

    Proof

    union(x,y) Operation

    Its actual cost is Θ(1)Θ(1), so we consider the potential change it causes.

    Assume 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) ≤𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑦)rnk(x)≤rnk(y), i.e. 𝑥x is attached to 𝑦y. Then the only nodes whose potential may increase are 𝑥x (from a root to a non-root), 𝑦y (rank may increase), and the children of 𝑦y before the operation (their parent's rank may increase). First prove that, before the operation, 𝑦y's child 𝑐c has potential that cannot increase, and if it decreases, it decreases by at least 11.

    Let the potential of 𝑐c before the operation be Φ(𝑐)Φ(c), and after the operation be Φ(𝑐′)Φ(c′). Here 𝑐c can be any non-root node with 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑐) >0rnk(c)>0, and the operation can be any operation, including the find operation below. We discuss three cases.

    1. Neither 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)iter(c) nor 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑐)level(c) increases. Clearly, Φ(𝑐) =Φ(𝑐′)Φ(c)=Φ(c′).
    2. 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)iter(c) increases, while 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑐)level(c) does not. Here 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)iter(c) increases by at least one, so Φ(𝑐′) ≤Φ(𝑐) −1Φ(c′)≤Φ(c)−1; the potential decreases by at least 1.
    3. 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑐)level(c) increases, and 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)iter(c) may decrease. However, since 0 <𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐) ≤𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑐)0<iter(c)≤rnk(c), 𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)iter(c) can decrease by at most 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑐) −1rnk(c)−1, while 𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑐)level(c) increases by at least 11. From the definition Φ(𝑐) =(𝛼(𝑛) −𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑐)) ×𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑐) −𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑐)Φ(c)=(α(n)−level(c))×rnk(c)−iter(c), we get Φ(𝑐′) ≤Φ(𝑐) −1Φ(c′)≤Φ(c)−1.
    4. Other cases. Since 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑐)rnk(c) is unchanged and 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑓𝑎(𝑐))rnk(fa(c)) does not decrease, such cases do not exist.

    Thus, the only nodes whose potential may increase are 𝑥x or 𝑦y. When 𝑥x changes from root to non-root, if 𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) =0rnk(x)=0, then Φ(𝑥) =Φ(𝑥′) =0Φ(x)=Φ(x′)=0 always. Otherwise, we must have 𝛼(𝑥) ×𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) ≥(𝛼(𝑛) −𝑙𝑒𝑣𝑒𝑙(𝑥)) ×𝑟𝑛𝑘(𝑥) −𝑖𝑡𝑒𝑟(𝑥)α(x)×rnk(x)≥(α(n)−level(x))×rnk(x)−iter(x). Therefore, Φ(𝑥′) ≤Φ(𝑥)Φ(x′)≤Φ(x).

    So the only node whose potential may increase is 𝑦y. The potential of 𝑦y increases by at most 𝛼(𝑛)α(n). Therefore, the amortized time complexity of the 𝑢𝑛𝑖𝑜𝑛union operation is Θ(𝛼(𝑛))Θ(α(n)).

    find(a) Operation

    If the search path contains Θ(𝑠)Θ(s) nodes, its actual time complexity is clearly Θ(𝑠)Θ(s). If no node's potential increases due to the find operation, and at least 𝑠 −𝛼(𝑛)s−α(n) nodes have potential decreased by at least 11, then the time complexity of 𝑓𝑖𝑛𝑑(𝑎)find(a) can be proven to be Θ(𝛼(𝑛))Θ(α(n)). To avoid confusion, we use 𝑎a as the parameter, while every 𝑥x below refers generically to a node in the DSU.

    First prove that no node's potential increases. This is obvious: we proved above that the potential of every non-root node does not increase, and the root's 𝑟𝑛𝑘rnk is unchanged, so no node's potential increases.

    Next prove that at least 𝑠 −𝛼(𝑛)s−α(n) nodes have potential decreased by at least 11.
